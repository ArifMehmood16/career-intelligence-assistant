# Licensing research — 2026-10-08

## Current human decision

The human chose open source to encourage contributions, asking that people know
Arif Mehmood started/created the project. This supersedes prior restrictive draft
policies on the same unmerged PR. Use creator credit for this project; do not
claim exclusive ownership of a general idea or independent implementations.

Decision: unchanged [official Apache License 2.0](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0.txt).
Section 2 grants copyright permissions, section 3 grants limited contributor patent
rights, section 4 governs redistribution/notices and section 5 incoming contributions.
Commercial use and private modifications are permitted under its terms. No extra
revenue, approval or UI-credit clause is inserted. NOTICE carries original
copyright, original creator and project link; README repeats visible creator credit.
The [Apache FAQ](https://www.apache.org/foundation/license-faq.html) explains reuse,
commercial use and keeping modifications private.

The [Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd) requires free redistribution
and no business-use prohibition. A custom external monetisation gate would conflict
with the latest human decision. Standard licensing also reduces the unfamiliar
terms that [GitHub's legal guide](https://opensource.guide/legal/) identifies as a
barrier to reuse. No extra CLA or assignment is needed for this bounded change;
contributors submit under Apache 2.0 and retain copyright.

Relevant notice preservation does not guarantee every hosted product shows a
creator's name in its UI. Apache 4(d) allows specified NOTICE placement options;
its contents are informational and do not modify the licence. Copyright protects
expression, not independent ideas; see the [Copyright Office FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html).
Original project creator credit is appropriate without a broader invention claim.
Earlier valid grants remain applicable to the versions offered under them.

Third-party fonts retain SIL OFL and vendored Spec Kit retains MIT. No runtime,
provider, dependency or private-data change; no professional legal review claimed.
